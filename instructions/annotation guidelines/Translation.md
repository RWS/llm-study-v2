Your job is to rate the translated text in three metrics: **Grammar** (syntax issues, misspellings, punctuation, etc.), **Naturalness** (vocabulary, tone, fluency etc.) and **Translation Accuracy** (how well it is rendered in the target language).

## Grammar

- A score of **5** is for perfect text with no issues.
- A score of **4** is for text containing a minor error which is commonly made by native speakers.
- A score of **3** is for text containing multiple minor errors or a single major error, something that you might expect from poorly educated people or perhaps a non-native speaker who's not perfectly fluent.
- A score of **2** is used for text containing many errors.
- A score of **1** is the lowest score used for cases when very poor grammar makes the language almost incomprehensible.

## Naturalness

- **5** is for text that reads very naturally, with no doubt it was written by a native speaker.
- **4** is for text which sounds natural but can be improved.
- **3** is for text that does not feel entirely natural - it may have been written by a poorly educated person or a non-native speaker.
- **2** is for text that mostly does not feel natural - it may have been written by someone who is just starting to learn the language.
- **1** is for text that sounds completely unnatural.

In addition to assessing Grammar and Naturalness of the translated sample you will be checking whether the translation:

- is adapted to the cultural preferences of the target audience,
- is consistent in the style, tone, terminology.

## Translation Accuracy

- **5** is for translation that is accurate, culturally adapted, and uses consistent style, tone, and terminology.
- **4** is for text that is rendered mostly correctly in the target language, but may contain not fully idiomatic language or inconsistent style. The meaning of the source text is represented accurately.
- **3** is for text which is generally rendered correctly, but it contains minor accuracy issues or multiple issues regarding idiomatic language or consistency in style.
- **2** is for translation that is mostly rendered incorrectly - it contains multiple accuracy issues.
- **1** is used when the text is not rendered correctly at all - the original meaning is lost.

  > **Note:** Do not use this flag for texts that include only a few words in another language. If you use this flag, you should still assess the paragraph as a whole, and assign a score of **1** for both Grammar and Naturalness. (It may be correct and natural in the other language, but not in the one being evaluated.)

- **Some words in the wrong language:** The text includes a word or a few words from another language that are not established loanwords and appear out of place.
